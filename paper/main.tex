\title{ISAC-Assisted Proactive Handover\\
in mmWave Networks: Value of Foresight\\
and Localization Requirements}

\author{
\IEEEauthorblockN{Seyed Bagher Hashemi Natanzi\IEEEauthorrefmark{1}\textsuperscript{,}\thanks{*Corresponding author: snatanzi@wpi.edu}, Bo Tang\IEEEauthorrefmark{2}}
\IEEEauthorblockA{\IEEEauthorrefmark{1}\IEEEauthorrefmark{2}Department of Electrical and Computer Engineering, Worcester Polytechnic Institute (WPI), USA}
\IEEEauthorblockA{Email: \{snatanzi, btang1\}@wpi.edu}
}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Millimeter-wave (mmWave) links are interrupted abruptly when bodies
cross the line of sight, and reactive handover recovers only after the
outage has begun. Integrated sensing and communication (ISAC) promises
to let the network see blockers coming and move users to another cell
in advance. This paper asks how much such foresight is worth relative to
the best reactive handover, and what sensing accuracy is needed to
harvest it. We formulate cost-aware cell selection with knowledge of the
future signal-to-noise ratio as a sequential decision problem, define
the value of foresight through a policy-independent bound, and develop
an ISAC-assisted handover framework in which a radio unit senses
blockers with its own OFDM waveform and a simulated Open RAN xApp
plans handovers. Ray-traced experiments in a \SI{28}{GHz} street canyon,
reported on held-out scenarios, show that perfect foresight closes most
of the gap between service-aware reactive handover and an ideal oracle
with a short look-ahead. Harvesting this value, however, requires the
user and nearby blockers to be localized to about a decimeter, well
beyond what our radar tracker and meter-level user positioning provide;
with realistic errors, even an uncertainty-aware planner largely defers
to reactive handover.
\end{abstract}

\begin{IEEEkeywords}
ISAC, mmWave blockage, proactive handover, O-RAN, ray tracing, digital twin.
\end{IEEEkeywords}

% ---------------------------------------------------------------------
\section{Introduction}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------------
The wide bandwidths of the millimeter-wave (mmWave) bands are central to
the capacity of 5G and 6G networks, but their short wavelength makes
links fragile: when a pedestrian or a vehicle crosses the line of sight
(LoS), the received power drops by tens of decibels within milliseconds
and stays low for up to seconds
\cite{rappaport2013mmwave,maccartney2017blockage}. Today's mobility
procedures are reactive. Beam failure recovery and handover are
triggered by degraded measurements, filtered over a time-to-trigger, so
the user experiences at least part of every outage. For services with
tight latency or rate requirements, such as extended reality or
connected vehicles, these interruptions dominate the user experience.

Proactive mitigation requires knowing the blocker before it arrives.
Prior work has predicted blockage and triggered proactive handoff from
cameras or dedicated radars
\cite{charan2021vision,demirhan2022radar,alkhateeb2023deepsense}, which
adds sensors, cost, and privacy concerns. Integrated sensing and
communication (ISAC) offers an alternative: the radio unit senses its
surroundings with its own OFDM waveform \cite{liu2022isac,sturm2011ofdm},
3GPP has studied ISAC use cases and standardized sensing-target channel
models \cite{3gpp22837,3gpp38901}, and Open RAN (O-RAN) allows an xApp to
act on such information through the E2 interface
\cite{polese2023oran,bonati2021intelligence}.

Whether this pays off depends on two quantities that are rarely
separated. The first is the \emph{value} of foresight: what a network
with perfect knowledge of the future could gain over the best reactive
handover. The second is the \emph{realizable} part: what a practical
predictor and policy actually deliver. Existing studies typically
report gains of a specific predictor over link-failure recovery or
untuned triggers, so neither quantity is known, and it remains unclear
which sensing accuracy a network would need. Measuring the value
requires a strong reactive baseline and a policy-independent bound;
measuring the realizable part requires a model in which the blocker that
the radar observes is the same object that attenuates the link.

This paper addresses both questions. Our contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
\item A formulation of cost-aware cell selection with knowledge of the
future signal-to-noise ratio (SNR), solved exactly by dynamic
programming, which yields a bound for any proactive policy and a
definition of the value of foresight relative to service-aware reactive
handover (3GPP A5).
\item An ISAC-assisted handover framework in which a radio unit senses
blockers with its own waveform, tracks them with digital-twin-aware
processing, predicts each cell's SNR, and plans handovers in an O-RAN
xApp (modelled in simulation), including an uncertainty-aware planner.
\item A spatially consistent simulation built on Sionna~RT~2.2
\cite{hoydis2023sionnart,sionna} in which every moving body is both a 3GPP
radar target and a blocker of communication paths, released as open
source.\footnote{\url{https://github.com/CLIS-WPI/sense2connect}}
\item Experimental findings on held-out scenarios: which blockers
matter, that foresight closes most of the improvable gap with a short
horizon if switches are planned with their cost in mind, and, through a
calibrated error analysis, the user and blocker localization accuracy
that foresight requires.
\end{itemize}
Section~II reviews related work, Section~III formulates the problem,
Section~IV presents the framework, Section~V reports the experiments,
and Sections~VI and~VII discuss and conclude.

\section{Related Work}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------------
\textit{mmWave blockage.} Measurements report deep and fast fading
caused by pedestrians at mmWave frequencies \cite{maccartney2017blockage},
and 3GPP models blockage with a stochastic model~A and a geometric
knife-edge model~B \cite{3gpp38901}. System-level studies mostly treat
blockers statistically, which decouples the blocker from anything the
network could observe.

\textit{Sensing-aided mobility.} Vision-aided methods predict blockage
and trigger proactive handoff from camera images
\cite{charan2021vision}, and radar-aided methods predict beams and
blockages from a co-located automotive radar
\cite{demirhan2022radar,demirhan2022radarblockage}, supported by
multi-modal datasets \cite{alkhateeb2023deepsense}. RaDaR
\cite{alquraan2023radar} equips each small cell with a separate FMCW
radar, uses object, base-station and user heights to decide whether an
object obstructs the LoS, and predicts the time to blockage, reporting
gains over a handover that starts only after link failure. Look Before
Switch \cite{li2025lookbefore} uses the ISAC capability of the gNB to
track the vehicular user itself and triggers handover from its estimated
position and maneuver, halving the handover interruption relative to A3.
We differ in three respects: we sense third-party blockers with the
communication waveform rather than the user or an auxiliary radar; we
compare against a tuned service-aware reactive policy (A5) and a
cost-aware oracle rather than against link-failure recovery or fixed A3
settings; and we separate the value of foresight from the part that
sensing realizes. Our finding that switching cost shapes what foresight
can achieve is complementary to interruption-reducing protocols such as
\cite{li2025lookbefore}.

\textit{ISAC and O-RAN.} OFDM radar processing with communication
waveforms is well established \cite{sturm2011ofdm} and ISAC is a key 6G
feature \cite{liu2022isac}. O-RAN research has demonstrated data-driven
control through xApps \cite{polese2023oran,bonati2021intelligence} on
open platforms such as OpenAirInterface and FlexRIC
\cite{nikaein2014oai,schmidt2021flexric}.

\subsection{Network, Channel, and Blockage}
We consider a set $\mathcal{C}$ of cells, each served by an O-RAN radio
unit (O-RU) with an $N$-element array, and a single-antenna user
equipment (UE)~$u$ that can be served by any cell in $\mathcal{C}$. The
channel from O-RU~$c$ to the UE at frequency $f$ and time $t$ is
\begin{equation}
h_{c}(f,t)=\sum_{\ell} 10^{-L_\ell(t)/20}\,\alpha_\ell(t)\,
\mathbf{a}_c(\theta_\ell)\,e^{-j2\pi f\tau_\ell(t)},
\label{eq:channel}
\end{equation}
where $\alpha_\ell$, $\tau_\ell$, and $\theta_\ell$ are the gain, delay,
and departure direction of path~$\ell$ without blockers, $\mathbf{a}_c$
is the array response, and $L_\ell(t)$ is the loss caused by the moving
bodies that cross the path. Following 3GPP blockage model~B
\cite{3gpp38901}, each body $k$ acts as a screen on every path segment it
intersects, with loss
\begin{equation}
L_k=-20\log_{10}\!\bigl(1-(F_{h_1}+F_{h_2})(F_{w_1}+F_{w_2})\bigr),
\label{eq:modelB}
\end{equation}
where each knife-edge term is
$F=\pi^{-1}\arctan\!\bigl(\pm\frac{\pi}{2}\sqrt{\pi\lambda^{-1}(D_1+D_2-r)}\bigr)$
and $D_1$, $D_2$, $r$ are the edge and direct distances of the segment;
losses add in decibels over bodies and segments. We denote by
$\gamma_c(t)$ the SNR of the best beam of cell~$c$ and by
$\bar\gamma_c(t)$ its value without blockers. The UE requires a rate
$R$, i.e., an SNR $\gamma_{\mathrm{req}}$, and is in \emph{outage}
whenever $\gamma_{c_t}(t)<\gamma_{\mathrm{req}}$ for its serving cell
$c_t$; every handover interrupts the user plane for $\tau_{\mathrm{HO}}$,
which also counts as outage.

\subsection{Sensing and Prediction Model}
The O-RUs transmit OFDM sensing symbols as part of their frames. With
$N_{\mathrm{sc}}$ subcarriers of spacing $\Delta f$ (bandwidth
$B=N_{\mathrm{sc}}\Delta f$) and a coherent processing interval of $M$
symbols spaced $T_s$, a monostatic radar resolves range, radial
velocity, and the maximum unambiguous velocity as
\begin{equation}
\Delta R=\frac{c}{2B},\qquad
\Delta v=\frac{\lambda}{2MT_s},\qquad
v_{\max}=\frac{\lambda}{4T_s}.
\label{eq:res}
\end{equation}
Each moving body is a sensing target with state $\mathbf{x}_k(t)$
(position, velocity, class). The network obtains estimates
$\hat{\mathbf{x}}_k$ from its tracker and a UE position estimate
$\hat{\mathbf{p}}_u$ with errors, and predicts each cell's SNR as
\begin{equation}
\hat\gamma_c(t)=\bar\gamma_c(t)-\hat L_c(t),
\label{eq:pred}
\end{equation}
where $\bar\gamma_c$ comes from the digital twin and $\hat L_c$ applies
\eqref{eq:modelB} to the predicted blocker trajectories on the line
between O-RU~$c$ and $\hat{\mathbf{p}}_u$.

\subsection{Handover Problem and the Value of Foresight}
A handover policy selects the serving cell $c_t\in\mathcal{C}$ at every
step $t$ of duration $\Delta t$ and is judged by its outage time
\begin{equation}
J(\{c_t\})=\Delta t\sum_t \mathbb{1}\bigl\{\gamma_{c_t}(t)<\gamma_{\mathrm{req}}
\;\lor\; t\in\mathcal{I}(\{c_t\})\bigr\},
\label{eq:viterbi}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{I}$ contains the $\tau_{\mathrm{HO}}/\Delta t$ steps
after each switch, so that an interrupted step counts once.
\emph{Reactive} policies use only past measurements: A3 hands over when
the neighbor's filtered reference signal received power (RSRP) exceeds
the serving one by an offset plus hysteresis for a time-to-trigger
\cite{3gpp38331}; A5 hands over when the serving cell falls below one
threshold and the neighbor exceeds another, which makes it aware of the
service requirement.

Two bounds frame what foresight can achieve. The \emph{instantaneous
oracle} selects the better cell at every step without delay or
interruption. The \emph{cost-aware oracle} knows $\gamma_c(t)$ for all
cells and minimizes \eqref{eq:viterbi} exactly by the Viterbi algorithm
over the cell and the remaining interruption; no policy with any
predictor can do better. We call the outage difference between the best
reactive policy and the instantaneous oracle the \emph{improvable gap},
and the share of it closed by the cost-aware oracle the \emph{value of
foresight}. A \emph{grid-aligned} variant restricted to the decision
times of a practical controller isolates the cost of that restriction.

\section{ISAC-Assisted Handover Framework}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------------
\textit{Sensing and tracking.} Per coherent processing interval, the
sensing O-RU forms range--Doppler--angle maps from the received echoes,
detects targets with two-dimensional cell-averaging CFAR, rejects ghost
detections that mirror real targets on building walls known from the
digital twin (image method), clusters detections with DBSCAN, and tracks
them with an extended Kalman filter on range, angles, and radial
velocity. Optionally, tracks are constrained to lane center lines
(vehicles) and sidewalks (pedestrians, with a free model for crossings).

\textit{Prediction and planning.} An xApp in the near-real-time RAN
intelligent controller, modelled in simulation, receives tracks over E2
with period $T_r$ and acts after a loop delay $\tau_{\mathrm{E2}}$. At each report it predicts
$\hat\gamma_c$ by \eqref{eq:pred}; the \emph{planner} minimizes
\eqref{eq:viterbi} on $\hat\gamma_c$ over a horizon $H$ and its decision
grid and applies the first decision after the loop delay. The
\emph{genie-planner} uses the true future SNR and isolates the effect of
prediction errors. For comparison, \emph{trigger} policies hand over
when a LoS blockage of the serving cell is predicted and the other cell
is predicted clear.

\textit{Uncertainty-aware planning.} The \emph{uncertainty-aware planner}
keeps A5 as default and intervenes only when the expected outage
reduction, averaged over $K$ samples drawn from the tracks' (inflated)
posterior and the UE position error, exceeds a threshold $\theta$.

\subsection{Setup}
\textit{Scenario.} We simulate an \SI{80}{m} street canyon (periodic,
constant traffic density) at $f_c=\SI{28}{GHz}$ with two cells
(Fig.~\ref{fig:scene}(a)). O-RU~0 is on the north side, on a lamppost
(\SI{5}{m}) or a facade (\SI{8}{m}); O-RU~1 is a \SI{5}{m} lamppost on the
south sidewalk, half a block away. Each O-RU has an $8\times8$
half-wavelength array; UEs carry one antenna at \SI{1.5}{m} on the south
sidewalk. Traffic consists of cars ($5\times2\times\SI{1.6}{m}$), buses
($12\times2.5\times\SI{3.2}{m}$), trucks ($8\times2.5\times\SI{3.5}{m}$),
and pedestrians who walk the sidewalks or cross the street; every moving
body is a TR~38.901 sensing target \cite{3gpp38901} (five scattering
points for vehicles, one for pedestrians). Communication paths are
traced without bodies and attenuated by \eqref{eq:modelB} every
$\Delta t=\SI{10}{ms}$; only the onset analysis resamples model~B at
\SI{1}{ms}.

\textit{Sensing.} O-RU~0 operates as a monostatic radar under an ideal
full-duplex assumption, transmitting on one element and receiving on its
array; Sionna's RCS solver traces paths through the scattering points.
One sensing symbol per slot (numerology~3, 1024 subcarriers) and a
32-ms processing interval give $\Delta R\approx\SI{1.2}{m}$,
$\Delta v\approx\SI{0.17}{m/s}$, and $v_{\max}\approx\SI{21}{m/s}$ in
\eqref{eq:res}; one interval every $T_r=\SI{0.1}{s}$ occupies
\numOverhead{} of the resources. The E2 loop delay is
$\tau_{\mathrm{E2}}=\SI{20}{ms}$ (modelled, without an implemented E2
service model), and the reported UE position carries a Gaussian error of
\SI{1}{m} per horizontal axis.

\textit{Link budget.} Transmit power (\SI{33}{dBm}) and UE noise figure
(\SI{13}{dB}) follow TR~38.802 \cite{3gpp38802}; unspecified losses
\cite{3gpp38830} are set to \SI{0}{dB}; the rate is Shannon's capped at
the highest NR spectral efficiency \cite{3gpp38214}. A rate of
\SI{400}{Mbit/s} requires $\gamma_{\mathrm{req}}=\numSNRreq$\,dB, and
$\tau_{\mathrm{HO}}=\SI{20}{ms}$. We define the link margin as the median
unblocked best-cell SNR minus $\gamma_{\mathrm{req}}$. The 3GPP budget
gives \numRefMargin\,dB on these short links; since longer links,
unspecified losses, or higher rates lower it, we sweep a common extra
path loss that sets the margin from 0 to \SI{30}{dB}.

\textit{Protocol.} All policies are tuned per margin on five tuning
seeds (A3 over offsets and hysteresis up to \SI{15}{dB}); methods were
fixed using ten development seeds, which also calibrate the tracker
error model below. All performance results come from a single final
evaluation on ten held-out seeds, each simulated for both mounts and
both traffic densities. Since the four runs of a seed share the traffic
and random draws, the seed is the statistical unit: we compare schemes
pairwise with the Wilcoxon signed-rank test over the ten seeds and give
95\% bootstrap confidence intervals over seeds.





\subsection{Which Blockers Matter}
Users experience about \numEventsPerMin{} LoS outage events
(\SI{10}{dB} model-B loss) per minute; Table~\ref{tab:classes}
summarizes them by blocker class. Cars never cause them: for a UE at
height $h_u$ and an O-RU at height $h_o$ and horizontal distance $d$, the
LoS height at horizontal distance $x$ from the UE is
$z(x)=h_u+(h_o-h_u)x/d$, i.e., \SI{2.7}{m}--\SI{3.3}{m} over the near
lane: above a car, but through a bus or truck (Fig.~\ref{fig:scene}(a)).
Pedestrians block near the UE end of the ray.

A blocker shadows the whole angular sector of the serving O-RU,

the loss caused by a bus or truck rises over \numOnsetBus\,s, whereas a
pedestrian next to the UE cuts the link almost at once:
\numOnsetPedFast{} of pedestrian onsets are shorter than \SI{10}{ms}.

\subsection{Sensing Performance}
With the detector tuned to about \numFA{} false alarms per processing
interval, the track detection probability is \numBusPd{} for buses and
trucks and \numPedPd{} for pedestrians, and a confirmed track exists
\SI{0.5}{s} before onset in \numLeadBus{} of heavy-vehicle and
\numLeadPed{} of pedestrian outages (Fig.~\ref{fig:leadtime}). Of three
uses of the digital twin, static-clutter subtraction is equivalent to
blind zero-Doppler removal, because static clutter has exactly zero
Doppler in simulation; image-method ghost rejection removes 14--21\% of
the unmatched clusters and, at a fixed false-alarm budget, raises
heavy-vehicle detection from \numGhostGain{}; and map-constrained
tracking raises detection from \numMapBus{} for heavy vehicles and from
\numMapPed{} for pedestrians. Sensing thus sees heavy vehicles reliably
and pedestrians only about half of the time: on the development seeds,
a pedestrian track is confirmed after a median of \numCalPedAcq~s and
then lost for \numCalPedOut~s at a time, and along-lane position errors
range from \numCalPosErr~m.

\subsection{The Value of Foresight}
Fig.~\ref{fig:results}(a) shows the outage versus margin. Service-aware
A5, the best reactive policy on the tuning seeds, is our reference; on
the held-out seeds it is on par with the wide-grid A3 at low margins,
and its advantage grows with the margin (\numAfiveRef{} vs.\
\numAthreeWideRef~s/UE-min at the reference), because A3 hands over on
RSRP differences even when the serving cell still meets the service rate.

The cost-aware oracle closes \numValueRange{} of the improvable gap at
every margin from 5\,dB up (Fig.~\ref{fig:results}(b)); at \SI{10}{dB} it
reaches \numCostOracleTen{} vs.\ \numAfiveTen{} for A5 and
\numOracleTen{} for the instantaneous oracle. It does so largely by
making fewer, better-timed switches, so it avoids interruption as well as
blockage. In absolute terms, perfect foresight lowers the outage of A5
by \numRelRedTen{} (\numAbsRedTen~s/UE-min) at \SI{10}{dB} and by
\numRelRedRef{} (\numAbsRedRef~s/UE-min) at the reference; most of the
remaining outage occurs when no cell is usable. High traffic density
raises the outage about threefold (A5: \numDensLowTen{} vs.\
\numDensHighTen~s/UE-min at \SI{10}{dB}). The genie-planner closes
\numGeniePlanShareRange{} of the gap (\numGeniePlanShareTen{} at
\SI{10}{dB}); against the grid-aligned oracle, which exceeds the bound by
at most \numGridCost, a horizon of \numPlanHorizon~s already suffices.
Trigger-based policies do not: even with perfect prediction, the trigger
genie stays at the level of the A3 it builds on (\numGenieTen{} vs.\
\numAthreeTen{} at \SI{10}{dB}), because blockage triggers ignore
sub-threshold losses and the cost of the switches they add. Foresight
therefore needs cost-aware planning, but little look-ahead.

Pedestrian blockage accounts for \numValuePedRange{} of the improvable
gap and heavy vehicles for \numValueBusRange{}; cars contribute only at
margins of 0--\SI{5}{dB}, where their smaller losses already cause
outage. This is the crux: the blockers that sensing observes well, heavy
vehicles, cause slow outages that A5 follows reactively, while most of
the value lies in abrupt pedestrian blockage near the UE, which the
radar tracks in only about half of the cases.

\subsection{What ISAC Delivers}
With predictions from the tracker, the planner falls behind A5 at every
margin (\numSensePlanTen{} vs.\ \numAfiveTen{} at \SI{10}{dB};
\numSensePlanRef{} vs.\ \numAfiveRef{} at the reference), with
\numSensePlanHO{} handovers per UE-minute against \numAfiveHO{} for A5
and a ping-pong rate of \numSensePlanPP: missed and false blockage
predictions translate into wrong switches. Fed with the true LoS loss
instead, while still paying the sensing overhead, the same planner has a
lower mean outage than A5 from \numTrueLossFrom\,dB upward
(\numTrueLossRef{} vs.\ \numAfiveRef{} at the reference); the advantage
is significant at \numTrueLossSigFrom--\SI{30}{dB} and at the reference
($p\le\numTrueLossSigMaxP$ each, without correction for the number of
margins) but not at \numTrueLossFrom\,dB ($p=\numPairedMaxP$).

(\numBEPosRef{} and \numBEUERef~m at the reference). These are
single-error thresholds, not guarantees under simultaneous errors; as
exploratory evidence (pointwise tests without correction), the advantage
remains significant only up to \numBEPosSigMax~m and \numBEUESigMax~m,

\SI{0.5}{m} wide, so errors of this size move it in or out of the
predicted line of sight; in this deployment and error model, the user's
own position is the binding constraint.

\textit{Uncertainty-aware decisions.} Accounting for the uncertainty
does not recover the gain either. Tuned on the tuning seeds ($K=\numUAK$,
no covariance inflation, $\theta=\numUAThetaTen$~s at \SI{10}{dB} and
\numUAThetaRef~s at the reference), the uncertainty-aware planner never
intervenes at 10, 15, 20, and \SI{30}{dB} and in at most
\numUAPlanIntervene{} of the epochs otherwise. It is significantly behind
A5 (\numUAPlanTen{} and \numUAPlanRef~s/UE-min at \SI{10}{dB} and the
reference). At \SI{10}{dB} this deficit is entirely the sensing overhead
(\numAfiveOHTen), since the planner takes the same decisions as A5; at
the reference, the overhead accounts for \numAfiveOHRef{} and the
residual difference from overhead-matched A5 (\numUAvsOHRef) is not
statistically significant. In our experiments, the tuned policy thus
rarely acts on the predictions.

% ---------------------------------------------------------------------
\section{Discussion and Limitations}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------------
In the studied deployment, the value of foresight concentrates in
pedestrian blockage close to the user, where both the blocker and the
user must be localized to about a decimeter, which exceeds what a
lamppost radar and meter-level user positioning provide. Making ISAC pay
off for handover therefore calls for precise user positioning, for
example from the same sensing infrastructure or from the UE, and for
sensing geometries that observe the user's surroundings from close by,
such as UE-side or bistatic sensing, and an implementation on an O-RAN
testbed such as OpenAirInterface with FlexRIC to measure the actual E2
loop delay. The study has limitations: a single
street canyon with two cells, an O-RAN control loop modelled by its
report period and delay rather than implemented on a platform such as
OpenAirInterface/FlexRIC, monostatic sensing at one radio unit with
ideal full duplex and isotropic array elements without a back plane
(which add mirror ghosts behind the radar), the knife-edge model~B
instead of full-wave diffraction, Shannon-based rates, a white Gaussian
model of the user position error, and an error model calibrated to our
tracker.

% ---------------------------------------------------------------------
\section{Conclusion}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------------
We studied ISAC-assisted proactive handover against mmWave blockage in
O-RAN. Against service-aware reactive handover, perfect foresight can
close most of the remaining outage gap with less than a second of
look-ahead, provided the policy plans switches with their cost in mind.
In the studied deployment and error model, harvesting this value
requires the user and nearby blockers to be localized to about a
decimeter: most of the value lies in abrupt pedestrian blockage next to
the user, and with realistic tracking and meter-level user positioning
even an uncertainty-aware planner defers to reactive handover. Precise
localization of the user and its immediate surroundings thus emerges as
a key prerequisite for ISAC to pay off in mmWave mobility.
