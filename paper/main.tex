\title{Revisiting ISAC-Enabled Proactive Handover for\\
Blockage-Resilient mmWave Open RAN}

\author{
\IEEEauthorblockN{Seyed Bagher Hashemi Natanzi\IEEEauthorrefmark{1}\textsuperscript{,}\thanks{*Corresponding author: snatanzi@wpi.edu}, Bo Tang\IEEEauthorrefmark{2}}
\IEEEauthorblockA{\IEEEauthorrefmark{1}\IEEEauthorrefmark{2}Department of Electrical and Computer Engineering, Worcester Polytechnic Institute (WPI), USA}
\IEEEauthorblockA{Email: \{snatanzi, btang1\}@wpi.edu}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Integrated sensing and communication (ISAC) promises to let a
millimeter-wave (mmWave) network see blockers coming and move users to
another cell before the link breaks. We ask how much such foresight is
worth relative to the best reactive handover, and how much of it
network-side ISAC can deliver.
Using a spatially consistent ray-traced model of a \SI{28}{GHz} street
canyon, in which the same 3GPP sensing targets scatter radar echoes and
obstruct communication paths, we build an end-to-end pipeline from
monostatic OFDM radar and digital-twin-aware tracking to a simulated
Open RAN handover xApp. We find that foresight is valuable but hard to harvest.
With perfect knowledge of the future signal-to-noise ratio, a cost-aware
planner closes \numValueRange{} of the gap between service-aware reactive
handover (3GPP A5) and an ideal oracle, and a horizon of \numPlanHorizon~s
suffices, whereas trigger-based policies fail to realize this value.
Most of the value, however, lies in
pedestrian blockage, which is abrupt and poorly observed from the
network, whereas heavy vehicles, tracked before onset in
\numLeadBus{} of cases, cause slow outages that reactive handover
already follows. With tracker-based predictions the planner therefore
falls behind A5, although with exact blockage knowledge it
significantly outperforms A5 at margins of \numTrueLossFrom~dB and
above. An error-injection analysis shows that position and velocity
errors of about \numFragNoise~m already erase the benefit of foresight,
whereas missed detections matter least. Decimeter-level localization of
blockers near the user, not the decision policy, is the bottleneck.
\end{abstract}

\begin{IEEEkeywords}
ISAC, mmWave blockage, proactive handover, O-RAN, ray tracing, digital twin.
\end{IEEEkeywords}

% ---------------------------------------------------------------------
\section{Introduction}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------------
Millimeter-wave links lose tens of decibels when a body crosses the line
of sight (LoS) \cite{rappaport2013mmwave,maccartney2017blockage}. A
natural remedy is foresight: if the network knew that a bus is about to
cross a user's LoS, it could hand the user over in advance. Prior work
has predicted blockage and triggered proactive handoff from cameras or
dedicated radars \cite{charan2021vision,demirhan2022radar,alkhateeb2023deepsense}.
Integrated sensing and communication (ISAC) removes the extra sensors:
the radio unit senses with its own OFDM waveform
\cite{liu2022isac,sturm2011ofdm}, 3GPP has studied ISAC use cases and
standardized sensing-target channel models \cite{3gpp22837,3gpp38901},
and Open RAN (O-RAN) lets an xApp act on such information through the
E2 interface \cite{polese2023oran,bonati2021intelligence}.

Whether this pays off depends on two quantities that are rarely
separated: the \emph{value} of foresight, i.e., what a network with
perfect knowledge of the future could gain over the best reactive
handover, and the \emph{realizable} part, i.e., what a practical
predictor and policy actually deliver. Measuring the first requires a
strong reactive baseline and a policy-independent bound; measuring the
second requires a model in which the blocker that the radar sees is the
same object that attenuates the link.

Our contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
\item A spatially consistent ISAC--blockage simulation built on
Sionna~RT~2.2 \cite{hoydis2023sionnart}: every moving body is a 3GPP
TR~38.901 radar target and, through blockage model~B, an obstacle for
communication paths. The pipeline is bitwise reproducible and released
as open source.\footnote{\url{https://github.com/CLIS-WPI/sense2connect}}
\item A characterization of which blockers matter: cars never obstruct
the LoS of \SI{5}{m}--\SI{8}{m} radio units, a blocker shadows every
path of the serving cell, and heavy-vehicle outages rise orders of
magnitude more slowly than pedestrian ones.
\item A value-of-foresight analysis with a cost-aware oracle that
bounds any proactive policy, against service-aware reactive handover
(A5), showing that foresight closes most of the remaining gap, needs
only a short horizon, and requires cost-aware planning rather than
blockage triggers.
\item A simulated end-to-end pipeline from the radar to a handover
xApp, with twin-aware ghost handling, map-constrained tracking, and a
receding-horizon planner, and an error-injection analysis that
quantifies the localization accuracy that foresight requires.
\end{itemize}

% ---------------------------------------------------------------------
\section{Related Work}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------------
\textit{mmWave blockage.} Measurements report deep and fast fading
caused by pedestrians at mmWave frequencies \cite{maccartney2017blockage},
and 3GPP models blockage with a stochastic model~A and a geometric
knife-edge model~B \cite{3gpp38901}. System-level studies mostly treat
blockers statistically, which decouples the blocker from anything the
network could observe.

\textit{Sensing-aided mobility.} Vision-aided methods predict blockage
and trigger proactive handoff from camera images
\cite{charan2021vision}, and radar-aided methods predict beams and
blockages from a co-located automotive radar
\cite{demirhan2022radar,demirhan2022radarblockage}, supported by
multi-modal datasets \cite{alkhateeb2023deepsense}. RaDaR
\cite{alquraan2023radar} equips each small cell with a separate FMCW
radar, uses object, base-station and user heights to decide whether an
object obstructs the LoS, and predicts the time to blockage, reporting
gains over a handover that starts only after link failure. Look Before
Switch \cite{li2025lookbefore} uses the ISAC capability of the gNB to
track the vehicular user itself and triggers handover from its estimated
position and maneuver, halving the handover interruption relative to A3.
We differ in three respects: we sense third-party blockers with the
communication waveform rather than the user or an auxiliary radar; we
compare against a tuned service-aware reactive policy (A5) and a
cost-aware oracle rather than against link-failure recovery or fixed A3
settings; and we separate the value of foresight from the part that
sensing realizes. Our finding that switching cost shapes what foresight
can achieve is complementary to interruption-reducing protocols such as
\cite{li2025lookbefore}.

\textit{ISAC and O-RAN.} OFDM radar processing with communication
waveforms is well established \cite{sturm2011ofdm} and ISAC is a key 6G
feature \cite{liu2022isac}. O-RAN research has demonstrated data-driven
control through xApps \cite{polese2023oran,bonati2021intelligence} on
open platforms such as OpenAirInterface and FlexRIC
\cite{nikaein2014oai,schmidt2021flexric}.

\section{System Model}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Deployment, Targets, and Mobility}
We model an \SI{80}{m} street canyon (periodic, constant traffic
density) at $f_c=\SI{28}{GHz}$ with two radio units (O-RUs) that form two
cells. O-RU~0 is on the north side, on a lamppost (\SI{5}{m}) or a
facade (\SI{8}{m}); O-RU~1 is a \SI{5}{m} lamppost on the south sidewalk,
half a block away. Each O-RU has an $8\times8$ half-wavelength array;
pedestrian user equipments (UEs) carry one antenna at \SI{1.5}{m} on the
south sidewalk. Traffic consists of cars ($5\times2\times\SI{1.6}{m}$),
buses ($12\times2.5\times\SI{3.2}{m}$), trucks ($8\times2.5\times\SI{3.5}{m}$),
and pedestrians who walk the sidewalks or cross the street. Every moving
body is a TR~38.901 sensing target \cite{3gpp38901} (five scattering
points for vehicles, one for pedestrians). Positions and speeds come
from random seeds; all parameters are tuned on five tuning seeds, and
results are reported on ten disjoint evaluation seeds with 95\%
confidence intervals. Schemes are compared pairwise on the same
evaluation runs with the Wilcoxon signed-rank test.

\subsection{Radar Channel and Processing}
O-RU~0 operates as a monostatic radar under an ideal full-duplex
assumption, transmitting on one element and receiving on its
$8\times8$ array. Sionna's RCS solver traces paths through the
scattering points, and the static background is traced separately. One
sensing OFDM symbol per slot (numerology~3, \SI{120}{kHz} spacing, 1024
subcarriers) gives a pulse repetition frequency of \SI{8}{kHz}, so a
coherent processing interval of 256 slots ($\SI{32}{ms}$) yields
\begin{equation}
\Delta R=\frac{c}{2B}\approx\SI{1.2}{m},\qquad
\Delta v=\frac{\lambda}{2MT_s}\approx\SI{0.17}{m/s},
\label{eq:res}
\end{equation}
and an unambiguous velocity $v_{\max}=\lambda f_{\mathrm{PRF}}/4\approx\SI{21}{m/s}$.
One interval is processed every \SI{0.1}{s}, so sensing occupies
\numOverhead{} of the resources. The chain comprises
range--Doppler--angle maps with thermal noise, two-dimensional
cell-averaging CFAR, image-method ghost rejection using the building
walls of the digital twin, DBSCAN clustering, and an extended Kalman
filter on range, angles, and radial velocity, optionally constrained to
lane center lines (vehicles) and sidewalks (pedestrians, with a free
model for crossings).

\subsection{Communication Channel, Blockage, and Margin}
Ray-traced targets are perfect absorbers, which would make every
blockage infinitely deep. We therefore trace the communication paths
without targets and attenuate every segment of every path with 3GPP
blockage model~B \cite{3gpp38901}, which treats blocker $k$ as a screen
with loss
\begin{equation}
L_k=-20\log_{10}\!\bigl(1-(F_{h_1}+F_{h_2})(F_{w_1}+F_{w_2})\bigr),
\label{eq:modelB}
\end{equation}
where each knife-edge term is
$F=\pi^{-1}\arctan\!\bigl(\pm\frac{\pi}{2}\sqrt{\pi\lambda^{-1}(D_1+D_2-r)}\bigr)$
and $D_1$, $D_2$, $r$ are the edge and direct distances of the segment.
Losses add in decibels over blockers and segments and are evaluated
every \SI{10}{ms} from the blockers' actual positions.

Transmit power (\SI{33}{dBm}) and UE noise figure (\SI{13}{dB}) follow
the dense-urban micro assumptions of TR~38.802 \cite{3gpp38802}; cable,
body, and implementation losses are left unspecified by 3GPP
\cite{3gpp38830} and set to \SI{0}{dB}. Each O-RU serves with its best
beam, and the rate is Shannon's capped at the highest NR spectral
efficiency \cite{3gpp38214}. A user is in outage when its SNR $\gamma$ is
below $\gamma_{\mathrm{req}}=\numSNRreq$\,dB, the value needed for
\SI{400}{Mbit/s}; every handover interrupts the user plane for
$\tau_{\mathrm{HO}}=\SI{20}{ms}$, counted as outage. We define the link
margin as the median unblocked best-cell SNR minus
$\gamma_{\mathrm{req}}$. The 3GPP budget gives \numRefMargin\,dB on these
short links; longer links, the unspecified losses, or higher rates
lower it, so we sweep a common extra path loss that sets the margin from
0 to \SI{30}{dB}.

\section{Handover Policies and Bounds}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------------
All policies are tuned separately at every margin on the tuning seeds.

\textit{Reactive.} \emph{A3} hands over when the neighbor's filtered
RSRP exceeds the serving one by an offset plus hysteresis for a
time-to-trigger \cite{3gpp38331}; we tune it over a wide grid (offsets
and hysteresis up to \SI{15}{dB}). \emph{A5} hands over when the serving
cell falls below one threshold and the neighbor exceeds another for a
time-to-trigger, which makes it aware of the service requirement.

\textit{Bounds.} The \emph{instantaneous oracle} selects the better cell
every \SI{10}{ms} without delay or interruption. The \emph{cost-aware
oracle} knows the future SNR $\gamma_c(t)$ of both cells and chooses the
cell sequence $\{c_t\}$, changeable at every step $\Delta t=\SI{10}{ms}$,
that minimizes the outage time
\begin{equation}
\sum_t \mathbb{1}\bigl\{\gamma_{c_t}(t)<\gamma_{\mathrm{req}}
\;\lor\; t\in\mathcal{I}(\{c_t\})\bigr\},
\label{eq:viterbi}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{I}$ contains the $\tau_{\mathrm{HO}}/\Delta t$ steps
after each switch, so that an interrupted step counts once, exactly as
in the evaluation. It is solved exactly by the Viterbi algorithm over
the cell and the remaining interruption; on every evaluation run, no
scheme does better. A \emph{grid-aligned} variant may switch only on the
planner's decision grid and exceeds this bound by at most
\numGridCost. We call the
outage gap between the best reactive policy and the instantaneous oracle
the \emph{improvable gap}, and the share of it closed by the cost-aware
oracle the \emph{value of foresight}.

\textit{Proactive.} The ISAC xApp is modelled in simulation: the E2
interface is represented by a \SI{0.1}{s} report period and a
\SI{20}{ms} loop delay, without an implemented E2 service model. Every
\SI{0.1}{s}, the xApp receives tracks and predicts each cell's SNR as
$\hat\gamma_c(t)=\bar\gamma_c(t)-\hat L_c(t)$, where $\bar\gamma_c$ is
the unblocked SNR from the digital twin and the UE position (known with
\SI{1}{m} error) and $\hat L_c$ is the model-B loss of the tracked
blockers on their predicted trajectories. The \emph{planner} solves
\eqref{eq:viterbi} on $\hat\gamma_c$ over a horizon $H$ and its
decision grid and applies the
first decision after the loop delay. The
\emph{genie-planner} uses the true future SNR instead. For comparison we
also evaluated \emph{trigger} policies that hand over when a LoS
blockage of the serving cell is predicted and the other cell is
predicted clear, both from tracks and from a perfect predictor (genie).

A blocker shadows the whole angular sector of the serving O-RU,
including the ground reflection: the best surviving path lies a median
\numSameCellLoss\,dB below the unblocked LoS, so same-cell beam
switching cannot recover the link and only a second cell can. That cell
is clear in most heavy-vehicle outages but in few pedestrian outages,
because a pedestrian close to the UE often shadows both cells. The
outages also differ sharply in dynamics (Fig.~\ref{fig:onset}): the loss
caused by a bus or truck rises over \numOnsetBus\,s, whereas a
pedestrian next to the UE cuts the link almost at once: \numOnsetPedFast{}
of pedestrian onsets are shorter than \SI{10}{ms}, and their median is at
the \SI{1}{ms} resolution of our evaluation.

\section{Sensing Performance}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------------
With the detector tuned to about \numFA{} false alarms per processing
interval, the track detection probability is \numBusPd{} for buses and
trucks and \numPedPd{} for pedestrians, and a confirmed track exists
\SI{0.5}{s} before onset in \numLeadBus{} of heavy-vehicle and
\numLeadPed{} of pedestrian outages (Fig.~\ref{fig:leadtime}). Of three
uses of the digital twin, static-clutter subtraction is equivalent to
blind zero-Doppler removal, because static clutter has exactly zero
Doppler in simulation; image-method ghost rejection removes 14--21\% of
the unmatched clusters and, at a fixed false-alarm budget, raises
heavy-vehicle detection from \numGhostGain{}; and map-constrained
tracking, added after the first results, raises detection from
\numMapBus{} for heavy vehicles and from \numMapPed{} for pedestrians,
and reduces the cross-lane velocity error from \numMapCross\,m/s.
Sensing thus sees heavy vehicles reliably and pedestrians only about
half of the time.

\section{The Value of Foresight and What ISAC Delivers}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------------
\textit{Reactive baseline.} Fig.~\ref{fig:outage} shows the outage
versus margin. Service-aware A5 is the best reactive policy at every
margin; its advantage over the wide-grid A3 grows with the margin
(\numAfiveRef{} vs.\ \numAthreeWideRef~s/UE-min at the reference),
because A3 hands over on RSRP differences even when the serving cell
still meets the service rate.

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\figorbox{fig_outage_margin.pdf}{3.6cm}{Outage vs.\ link margin.}
\caption{Outage time versus link margin for A3, A5, the sensing-driven
planner, the genie-planner, and the two oracles (95\% CI).}
\label{fig:outage}
\end{figure}

\textit{Value of foresight.} The cost-aware oracle closes
\numValueRange{} of the improvable gap at every margin from 5\,dB up
(Fig.~\ref{fig:value}); at \SI{10}{dB} it reaches \numCostOracleTen{}
vs.\ \numAfiveTen{} for A5 and \numOracleTen{} for the instantaneous
oracle. It does so largely by making fewer, better-timed switches, so it
avoids interruption as well as blockage. In absolute terms, perfect
foresight lowers the outage of A5 by \numRelRedTen{}
(\numAbsRedTen~s/UE-min) at \SI{10}{dB} and by \numRelRedRef{}
(\numAbsRedRef~s/UE-min) at the reference: most of the remaining outage
occurs when no cell is usable, which no mobility decision can avoid.
High traffic density roughly doubles the outage (A5: \numDensLowTen{}
vs.\ \numDensHighTen~s/UE-min at \SI{10}{dB}), while the relative value
of foresight is similar at \SI{10}{dB} and larger at low density at the
reference (\numRelRedRefLow{} vs.\ \numRelRedRefHigh). The genie-planner approaches this
bound (\numGeniePlanTen{} at \SI{10}{dB}); against the grid-aligned oracle,
which shares its decision grid, a horizon of \numPlanHorizon~s already
suffices. Trigger-based policies do not: even
with perfect prediction, the trigger genie stays at the level of the
A3 it builds on (\numGenieTen{} vs.\ \numAthreeTen{} at \SI{10}{dB}),
because blockage
triggers ignore sub-threshold losses that already cause outage and the
cost of the switches they add. Foresight therefore needs cost-aware
planning, but little look-ahead.

\textit{Where the value lies.} Pedestrian blockage accounts for
\numValuePedRange{} of the improvable gap and heavy vehicles for
\numValueBusRange{} (Table~\ref{tab:classes}). Cars, which never cause
\SI{10}{dB} LoS outages, contribute only at margins of 0--\SI{5}{dB},
where their smaller LoS losses already cause outage (Fig.~\ref{fig:value}). This is the crux: the
blockers that sensing observes well, heavy vehicles, cause slow outages
that A5 follows reactively, while most of the value lies in abrupt
pedestrian blockage near the UE, which the network-side radar tracks in
only about half of the cases.

\textit{What ISAC delivers.} With predictions from the tracker, the
planner falls behind A5 at every margin (\numSensePlanTen{} vs.\
\numAfiveTen{} at \SI{10}{dB}; \numSensePlanRef{} vs.\ \numAfiveRef{} at
the reference), with \numSensePlanHO{} handovers per UE-minute against
\numAfiveHO{} for A5 and a ping-pong rate of \numSensePlanPP: missed and
false blockage predictions translate directly into wrong switches. Fed
with the true LoS loss instead, while still paying the sensing overhead,
the same planner outperforms A5 at every margin from
\numTrueLossFrom\,dB upward (\numTrueLossRef{} vs.\ \numAfiveRef{} at the
reference; paired $p\le\numPairedMaxP$ at each of these margins, which
also holds under a Bonferroni correction over the margins). At lower
margins, the sensing overhead and the approximation of the SNR by the
LoS loss outweigh the benefit.

\textit{Error budget.} To attribute the shortfall, we start from perfect
tracks, which reproduce the true-loss result, and inject one error type
at a time at the rates measured for our tracker (Table~\ref{tab:errors});
all injections were added after an external review. Position and
velocity errors are the largest single cause from 5 to \SI{30}{dB},
false tracks dominate at the highest margins, and missed detections
matter least; combined, the errors interact and reproduce the observed
collapse. The advantage of perfect tracks already vanishes at a
\numFragMiss{} miss rate, at the measured false-track rate, or at
\numFragNoise~m and \numFragNoise~m/s of error: a pedestrian is about
\SI{0.5}{m} wide, so such an error moves it in or out of the LoS. A
guarded variant in which A5 runs by default and the planner may only
advance or veto switches based on long-confirmed tracks is also
significantly worse than A5 at every margin (\numRobustTen~s/UE-min at
\SI{10}{dB}). The policy is therefore not the obstacle; network-side
sensing must localize near-user blockers to a few decimeters before
foresight pays off.

% ---------------------------------------------------------------------
\section{Discussion and Limitations}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------------
Our results point to where ISAC-based mobility should improve. The
value concentrates in pedestrian blockage close to the user, which a
radar on a lamppost sees at short range, small radar cross-section and
often in the shadow of the user's own environment, while the error
budget asks for decimeter-level accuracy; UE-side or bistatic sensing
between radio units, a second radar at the other cell, and denser
deployments are natural next steps. The study has limitations: a single
street canyon, monostatic sensing with ideal full duplex, the knife-edge
model~B instead of full-wave diffraction, Shannon-based rates, UE
positions known to the network, and policies added after earlier
results or after an external review (map-constrained tracking, A5, the
planner, the grid-aligned oracle, the error injection, and the guarded
planner), which we report as such.

% ---------------------------------------------------------------------
\section{Conclusion}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------------
We revisited ISAC-enabled proactive handover against mmWave blockage
in O-RAN. Against service-aware reactive handover, perfect foresight
can still close most of the remaining outage gap and needs only a
fraction of a second of look-ahead, provided the policy plans switches
with their cost in mind. The value, however, lies mostly in pedestrian
blockage near the user, which network-side sensing predicts poorly,
whereas the heavy vehicles it tracks well cause outages that reactive
handover already follows. Errors of a few decimeters in the position of
near-user blockers suffice to erase the benefit, so accurate near-user
sensing is the key to making ISAC pay off for mmWave mobility.
